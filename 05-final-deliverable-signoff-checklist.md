# Final Deliverable Sign-off Checklist

Datimpacto · MEL Community of Practice Toolkit · Tool 5

Purpose: decide whether a final deliverable is ready to approve, with reasons you can share with the consultant/provider.
When to use: when the draft final deliverable arrives, and again for the revised version.
Time needed: 1.5 to 3 hours, depending on length.

## Seven Questions Before You Sign Off

| #   | Question                                            | What to look for                                                                                                                                                             | Red flag                                                                                                     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Does it answer what was asked?                      | Every priority question or ToR requirement is addressed.                                                                                                                     | Requirements dropped or merged without explanation.                                                          |
| 2   | Is the product trustworthy?                         | Sample achieved vs planned, response rates, missing data and limitations stated for a report; data lineage, coverage and known gaps stated for a dataset or system handover. | No mention of what went wrong, or what the deliverable does not cover.                                       |
| 3   | Is the underlying work sound?                       | Methods match the questions or requirements; comparisons, disaggregation or technical choices are explained.                                                                 | Percentages with no base, claims the design cannot support, or technical choices left unexplained.           |
| 4   | Are the findings or outputs credible?               | Each finding or output is backed by more than one source, or clearly labeled as based on one.                                                                                | Findings or outputs resting on a single quote, a single site or an unverified source.                        |
| 5   | Do conclusions follow from the evidence?            | You can trace each conclusion or design choice back to specific evidence.                                                                                                    | New ideas or choices that never appear in the evidence presented.                                            |
| 6   | Is the deliverable usable?                          | Each recommendation, indicator or component says who should do what, and by when, and links to a conclusion or requirement.                                                  | "Strengthen coordination" with no owner or action, or a component no one can operate without the consultant. |
| 7   | Is it readable by the team that has to run with it? | An executive summary or handover note a busy reader can use on its own; plain language; annexes or technical documentation for detail.                                       | A 100-page report with a 10-page summary, or documentation only the original consultant can navigate.        |

For each question, mark Meets / Partly meets / Does not meet, with a page or section reference.

Framed here for a final evaluation report, the toolkit's running example. The same seven questions apply to a MEL system handover, a dataset, or a design document.

## Decision

- Approve.
- Approve with minor revisions (list them; no new review round needed).
- Return for revision (list what must change before the next review).

Share comments in a response matrix (comment, section, provider response, status) so every point gets closed.

## A Note on AI

An AI assistant can run a first pass on the deliverable's text and stated methodology (see Tool 9). It does not see raw beneficiary-level data, and it does not approve the deliverable. A person reads it and signs off.
