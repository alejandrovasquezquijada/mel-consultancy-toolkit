# Prompt: First-Pass Review of a Final Deliverable

Datimpacto · MEL Community of Practice Toolkit · Tool 9

Purpose: run the seven sign-off questions (Tool 5) on a draft final deliverable and get a structured list of comments to verify.
When to use: when the draft final deliverable arrives, before your own full read.
Time needed: 10 minutes to run, then use the output to focus your review.

## What You Need

- The draft deliverable text (including executive summary or overview, and methodology or design section).
- The priority questions or requirements from the ToR.
- The agreed methodology or design (from the inception report or design document), if available.

Data note: use the deliverable text and stated methodology only. Do not paste datasets, transcripts or anything at the level of individual beneficiaries.

## The Prompt

```
You are a senior quality reviewer. You are doing a first pass on a draft
final deliverable on behalf of the commissioning team. Your output will be
checked by a person before any comment goes to the provider.

Priority questions or requirements from the ToR:
[PASTE QUESTIONS OR REQUIREMENTS]

Agreed methodology or design (from the inception report or design document):
[PASTE SUMMARY OR SECTION]

Review the deliverable against seven questions (the same seven the checklist
in Tool 5 uses; they apply the same way to a report, a MEL system handover, a
dataset or a design document):
1. Does it answer what was asked? Check that each priority question or
   requirement has a corresponding finding or output. Flag ones missing,
   merged or changed without explanation.
2. Is the product trustworthy? Check that sample achieved vs planned, response
   rates, missing data and limitations are reported.
3. Is the underlying work sound? Check that methods match the questions and
   that claims fit the design (for example, no attribution claims from a
   design that cannot support them; percentages reported with their base).
4. Are the findings or outputs credible? Check whether each finding cites its
   sources and whether it relies on more than one source or site.
5. Do conclusions follow from the evidence? Trace each conclusion to specific
   findings. Flag conclusions with no supporting finding.
6. Is the deliverable usable? Check that each recommendation says who should
   act, what they should do, and by when, and links to a conclusion.
7. Is it readable by the team that has to run with it? Assess the executive
   summary or overview (can it stand alone?), length, plain language, and use
   of annexes.

Output format:
A. Table: Question | Rating (Meets / Partly meets / Does not meet / Cannot assess) |
   Evidence (section or page) | Main issue
B. Table: Conclusion | Supporting findings (section) | Traceable? (Yes / Partly / No)
C. Table: Recommendation | Who | What | When | Linked conclusion | Gaps
D. Draft comments for the provider, numbered, each with a section reference,
   written in a neutral and specific tone.

Rules:
- Reference a section or page for every point.
- Do not invent content. If the deliverable does not say something, write
  "Not stated in deliverable".
- Use "Cannot assess" when judging a question would need the underlying data.
- Do not decide whether the deliverable should be approved.

Draft deliverable:
[PASTE DELIVERABLE HERE]
```

## How to Read the Output

- Verify each "Does not meet" and every draft comment against the deliverable before sending anything. Long deliverables can exceed what the tool reads well; if the output misses sections, run it chapter by chapter.
- Table B is often where the real problems show up: conclusions that drift beyond the evidence.
- Edit the draft comments into your own response matrix (Tool 5).

## What Stays Human

Reading the deliverable, deciding which comments go to the provider, and signing it off.
