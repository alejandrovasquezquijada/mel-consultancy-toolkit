# Prompt: Change Request Impact Assessment

Datimpacto · MEL Community of Practice Toolkit · Tool 8

Purpose: estimate the impact of a proposed change to scope, timeline or budget before the commissioning team decides on it.
When to use: whenever a change request (Tool 4, Part 2) is filed, before the decision is made.
Time needed: 5 minutes to run, 15 minutes to review.

## What You Need

- The draft change request (Tool 4).
- The original ToR scope, timeline and budget sections.
- Any relevant entries from the risk and issues log (Tool 4, Part 1).

Data note: use the change request text, ToR sections and aggregated figures only. Do not paste raw beneficiary-level data.

## The Prompt

```
You are supporting a commissioning team in assessing a proposed change to an
external consultancy. You do not approve or reject the change. You lay out its
impact so the team can decide.

Type of change: [ ] Scope   [ ] Timeline   [ ] Budget   [ ] More than one

Change requested:
[PASTE THE CHANGE REQUEST TEXT]

Original ToR scope, timeline and budget:
[PASTE RELEVANT SECTIONS]

Relevant open items from the risk and issues log, if any:
[PASTE OR SUMMARIZE]

Task:
1. Restate the change in one sentence, in plain language.
2. Assess the impact on each ToR element the change touches: priority
   questions or deliverables, geographic or methodological scope, timeline,
   and budget. Be specific about what moves and by how much.
3. Flag any ToR element not mentioned in the request that the change is likely
   to affect anyway (for example, a timeline extension that pushes into a
   holiday period or a permit renewal).
4. List the trade-offs of approving the change as requested versus approving
   it with conditions versus rejecting it.
5. Note whether this change, combined with any open items in the risk and
   issues log, suggests a pattern worth a milestone-level conversation rather
   than a one-off approval.
6. Draft the "Impact on priority questions or requirements," "Impact on
   timeline" and "Impact on budget" fields of the change request form
   (Tool 4) in a form the team can edit directly.

Output format:
A. One-sentence restatement of the change.
B. Table: ToR element | Impact | Magnitude (Low / Medium / High)
C. Elements affected but not mentioned in the request
D. Trade-offs: approve as requested / approve with conditions / reject
E. Pattern flag (yes/no, with reasoning)
F. Draft text for the change request form's impact fields

Rules:
- Base every point on the documents provided. If something needed for the
  assessment is missing, say so and list what is needed.
- Do not recommend which option the team should choose.
```

## How to Read the Output

- Section C is often the most useful part: changes rarely stay contained to the element the requester named.
- Section E matters most when several small change requests have landed close together. A pattern is a milestone-review conversation (Tool 3), not another quick approval.
- Use section F as a starting draft for Tool 4's form, not a final version.

## What Stays Human

Whether to approve, approve with conditions, or reject the change, and any renegotiation of scope, timeline or budget that follows.
